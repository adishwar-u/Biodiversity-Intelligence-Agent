Conservation tillage trials across Indian cropping systems consistently
show two effects that operate on different timescales. In the short term
(within one to two seasons), leaving crop residue on the surface instead of
incorporating or burning it reduces measured soil loss by 30-50% relative
to conventional tillage, because the residue layer absorbs raindrop impact
energy and slows overland flow. The same residue layer also buffers surface
soil temperature, typically 2-4 C cooler than bare tilled soil, and reduces
evaporative moisture loss between irrigations by 10-20%.

The longer-term effect — a 0.1-0.2% absolute rise in topsoil organic
carbon over roughly five years — comes from reduced disturbance of soil
aggregates, which slows the oxidation of organic matter that tillage
otherwise exposes to air.

The main adoption barrier documented is not the practice itself but the
capital cost of no-till seeding equipment, plus a higher weed burden in the
first two to three transition years before residue cover suppresses weed
germination.
