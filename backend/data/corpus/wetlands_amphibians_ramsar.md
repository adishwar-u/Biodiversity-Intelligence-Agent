---
title: "Wetlands and Biodiversity: An Overview"
org: Ramsar Convention Secretariat
year: 2018
topic: biodiversity
---

Amphibians are among the most reliable indicator taxa for wetland
restoration success because their life cycle depends directly on standing
or near-surface water persisting long enough for eggs to hatch and larvae
to metamorphose. Restored seasonal ponds and farm wetlands typically show
measurable amphibian recolonisation — new species detected at a site —
within two to three breeding seasons of re-wetting, well before slower
taxa like woody vegetation or larger vertebrates show comparable recovery.

Because amphibian presence responds quickly and sensitively to moisture
persistence, amphibian richness is a useful early proxy for whether a
water-retention intervention (bunding, ponds, check dams) is functioning
as intended, ahead of slower-to-measure outcomes like groundwater levels
or vegetation recovery.

Restored wetlands also anchor a broader food web: waterbirds, dragonflies
and other wetland-dependent invertebrates follow amphibian and plant
recolonisation, so wetland restoration tends to raise overall site species
richness beyond the wetland-specialist taxa alone.
