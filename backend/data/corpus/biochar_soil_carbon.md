---
title: "Biochar for Sustainable Soil Management"
org: CGIAR
year: 2019
topic: soil
---

Biochar — organic material pyrolysed at high temperature in low oxygen —
behaves differently from fresh compost or residue once in soil. Its
chemical structure resists microbial breakdown, so carbon added as biochar
persists in soil for decades rather than the few years typical of raw
organic amendments. Field trials report absolute soil organic carbon
increases of 0.2-0.4% that remain measurable ten or more years after a
single application, an unusually durable result compared to most
carbon-building practices.

Beyond carbon persistence, biochar's porous physical structure directly
increases water-holding capacity — trial plots typically show a 10-15%
increase relative to unamended controls — because the pore structure holds
water and nutrients that would otherwise leach through sandy or degraded
soils.

Outcomes are highly dependent on feedstock type and pyrolysis conditions,
and results vary meaningfully across soil textures, which is why the
literature treats biochar's biodiversity and yield co-benefits as
moderate-confidence rather than high-confidence, even where the carbon
persistence result itself is well established. Production also requires
a pyrolysis unit and a reliable feedstock supply chain, a real barrier to
smallholder adoption at scale.
