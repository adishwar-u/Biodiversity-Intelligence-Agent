Integrated watershed development — contour bunding, farm ponds and check
dams working together across a catchment — is one of the most extensively
studied land intervention packages in semi-arid India. Treated watersheds
consistently show higher soil moisture retention, higher open-well water
levels in the following dry season, and reduced peak runoff during monsoon
storms compared to untreated control catchments.

Check dams placed across seasonal drainage lines slow flow velocity enough
for suspended sediment to settle before water moves downstream, which both
protects downstream reservoirs from siltation and gives the water more
residence time to infiltrate locally. Farm ponds fed by bunded runoff extend
the period of standing or near-surface water well into the dry season,
which measurably increases sightings of amphibians and wetland-associated
birds at treated sites relative to controls within two to three years.

The main documented tradeoff is land take: bunds and ponds typically remove
3-5% of a farm's cultivable area from production, a real cost that farmers
weigh against the water-security benefit.
