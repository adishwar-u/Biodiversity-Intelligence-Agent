---
title: "Climate Change and Land: Special Report"
org: IPCC
year: 2019
topic: climate
---

Land degradation reduces the resilience of agricultural and natural systems
to climate variability, and this relationship runs in both directions: a
degrading climate accelerates land degradation, and degraded land is less
able to buffer climate shocks like drought or heavy rainfall.

Soil and vegetation-based interventions — agroforestry, conservation
agriculture, improved grazing management — deliver climate adaptation and
mitigation co-benefits simultaneously. They increase soil carbon
sequestration (mitigation) while also increasing the water-holding and
infiltration capacity of the land (adaptation to drought and flood extremes).

The report is explicit that single-variable framing of land interventions
understates their value: a practice justified purely on carbon grounds, or
purely on yield grounds, misses the cascade of secondary effects on water
balance, biodiversity and local microclimate that typically accompanies it.
Land management decisions should be evaluated across multiple environmental
outcomes at once, not one metric in isolation.
