title: "Agroforestry and Biodiversity Conservation in Tropical Landscapes"

Adding scattered or boundary trees to cropland or pasture is one of the
highest biodiversity-return-per-hectare interventions available in working
agricultural landscapes, because it adds a vertical habitat layer that
open cropland or grassland cannot provide on its own. Bird species counts
on tree-integrated farms typically run 20-40% higher than on treeless
fields in the same landscape, driven by added nesting sites and insect
prey associated with bark and canopy.

The canopy effect on microclimate is a secondary but important mechanism:
shaded soil surfaces run 3-6 C cooler than exposed soil under similar
conditions, which slows the rate of organic matter oxidation and helps
soil carbon accumulate faster under tree cover than in adjacent open field
soil.

Silvopasture — integrating trees directly into grazing systems — captures
this benefit for livestock producers, though it comes with a genuine
tradeoff of 10-20% reduced grazeable area under mature canopy and a
multi-year establishment period during which young trees need protection
from browsing animals.
