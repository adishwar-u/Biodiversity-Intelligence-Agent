---
title: "Biological Pest Control in Agroecosystems"
org: FAO
year: 2019
topic: biodiversity
---

Natural enemies of crop pests — predatory beetles, spiders, parasitoid
wasps, insectivorous birds — need two things a simplified monoculture
field rarely provides across a full season: a refuge from tillage and
harvest disturbance, and an alternative prey or floral resource during
periods when the target pest is scarce. Beetle banks, field margins and
structurally diverse vegetation supply exactly this, which is why fields
adjacent to such features consistently show higher predatory arthropod
density — often 30-70% higher near the refuge — than fields without them.

Insectivorous birds add a second trophic layer to this effect: bird
exclusion experiments (caging out birds from crop plots) show measurably
higher insect pest damage than uncaged controls, confirming birds
contribute pest suppression independent of insect predators.

The downstream consequence is agronomic, not just ecological: fields with
higher natural enemy abundance show reduced variance in yield loss from
pest outbreaks year to year, because a resident predator population
responds to a local pest population increase faster than a reactive
pesticide application can.
