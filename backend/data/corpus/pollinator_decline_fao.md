---
title: "Pollinators, Pollination and Food Production"
org: FAO
year: 2016
topic: biodiversity
---

Pollinator decline is driven less by any single cause than by the loss of
continuous floral resources across the growing season. Landscapes dominated
by a single synchronously-flowering crop offer pollinators a brief glut of
forage followed by a long resource gap, which drives colony decline and
lowers wild pollinator abundance even where pesticide exposure is low.

Interventions that extend flowering duration — drought-resilient
vegetation that delays senescence, staggered-flowering intercrops, or
retained semi-natural habitat with sequential bloom — directly address this
resource-gap mechanism. Farms adjacentto semi-natural habitat (hedgerows,
field margins, woodlots) show consistently  higher wild pollinator visitation
and, in insect-pollinated crops, measurably higher and more stable yields
than farms in simplified, margin-free landscapes.

Pollination-dependent crop yield stability should be treated as a downstream
consequence of habitat and flowering continuity, not as an independent
variable to be managed on its own.
